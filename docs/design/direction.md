# Ratio interface

Ratio is a desktop issue workbench for people who maintain their own software. The issue list is the first view after connecting a device. A project key identifies the work, the status column shows its state, and the selected issue brings the next action and evidence into focus. Luma is the sample project.

The desktop frame sits against a continuous fade from near-black at the top to silver-gray along its lower sides. A thin upper edge catches the light on the workspace. Inside, the rail, document and current selection use three neutral fills. A small margin mark picks out the selected issue. Monochrome increases text and divider contrast. In both appearances, shapes and labels distinguish status and graph series. The logo is a geometric R with a fraction slash cut into its stem.

The principal screens are the issue list and an issue’s description, activity and properties. Projects offer real progress from recorded issues; release curves and cycle-time plots open their source records. New accounts start empty and can create their own data. The separate Luma seed is clearly labeled.

## Design review

Check the rendered interface at desktop window size and narrow widths. Inspect row alignment, active/focused/hover states, disclosure and shortcut behavior, issue filters, the selected project, chart affordances and the sign-in screen. Keep the next action visible. Avoid promotional headings, decorative charts and duplicate success indicators.
